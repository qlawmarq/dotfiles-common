# How the no-claims discount works (insurer's internal primer, excerpt)

The no-claims discount, sometimes called a no-claims bonus, is the mechanism that rewards a policyholder for going a full policy year without making a claim. Each claim-free year moves the policyholder one step up a ladder of discount levels. A typical ladder runs from zero to five years, with the discount climbing from nothing at step zero to somewhere around sixty percent at the top. The exact percentages live in the rating tables and vary by product, so the ladder should be thought of as a shape rather than a fixed set of numbers.

A claim does not necessarily wipe the discount out. What happens depends on fault. When the policyholder is at fault, or when fault cannot be established, the ladder drops by two steps at the next renewal. When a third party is clearly at fault and we recover our costs in full, the discount is untouched. Windscreen claims and claims under certain add-on covers are carved out entirely and do not touch the ladder at all.

Policyholders can protect the discount by paying an additional premium. Protection does not freeze the premium; it freezes the discount step. A protected policyholder who makes an at-fault claim keeps their step, but the underlying premium can still rise because the claim itself feeds into the risk rating. This is the single most common source of complaints, because customers read "protected" as "my price cannot go up". It cannot be stressed enough that the discount and the base premium are two separate levers.

Protection usually carries a cap: for example, two at-fault claims in three years before the protection lapses and the ladder drops as normal. The cap resets on a rolling basis, so a claim from four years ago no longer counts against the policyholder.

When a policyholder switches insurers, the discount travels with them. The new insurer asks for proof of the no-claims history, which the previous insurer is obliged to provide within a set period. Most insurers honour up to five years, and some honour more. Gaps matter: if the policyholder has been off the road for more than two years, the history generally expires and the ladder starts again from zero.

Finally, the discount is attached to the policyholder, not the vehicle. Adding a second car does not double the discount; some products offer an introductory "mirrored" discount on the second car, but that is a marketing decision, not an entitlement, and it is handled outside the ladder.
